Have you ever sat down in a café to get something done — and actually done it? The task that had been waiting at home for a week, finished in an afternoon, with strangers at the next table, a barista in the background, and nobody paying you the slightest attention.

There's a name for that: **body doubling**.

## What body doubling is

Body doubling simply means working in the presence of another person. They don't help with your task. They don't have to talk to you, or even know what you're doing. They just need to be there, doing their own thing.

The term became popular in ADHD communities, where many people found that having someone nearby made it much easier to start tasks and stay with them. But you don't need a diagnosis to feel it. Libraries, study halls, co-working spaces and that one café with the good light all run on the same quiet magic.

## Why it works

Nobody fully understands every piece of it, but a few things seem to be going on.

**A soft sense of accountability.** When someone else is working, you feel a gentle pull to work too. Not pressure, exactly — it would just feel a little odd to be the only one scrolling.

**The room sets the mood.** We're social creatures, and we pick up on the rhythm of the people around us. A room full of quiet concentration makes concentrating feel like the natural thing to be doing.

**Work stops feeling so lonely.** Working alone for long stretches can feel isolating, especially if you work from home. Company, even silent company, takes the edge off. The task is still yours, but you're not doing it alone.

**It gives you a clear beginning.** Arriving somewhere, sitting down near someone, opening your notebook — these are small signals that it's time to start.

## Ways to try it

- **Go somewhere.** A library, a café, a co-working space. Bring one task, not ten.
- **Ask a friend.** Set up a work date: you each bring your own thing and work side by side for an hour. Talk over coffee afterwards.
- **Do it on a call.** Some friends keep a video call open while they each work on mute. It sounds odd, and works surprisingly well.
- **Share a quiet online room.** If you'd rather not be on camera, you can still know that someone else is there.

> **Try this this week:** Pick one task you've been avoiding and plan to do it in company — a friend, a library, or an online room. Before you start, say out loud (or just to yourself) what you're going to work on.
