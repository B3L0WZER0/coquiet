Five minutes between tasks. You pick up your phone "just to check something", and suddenly you're three videos deep, twelve minutes have gone, and you feel more tired than before.

That isn't a character flaw. Phones are designed to be the easiest thing to reach for. But they rarely give us what a break is meant to give: a moment of actual rest.

## What a break is for

When you've been focusing, your attention has been working hard in one direction, usually at one distance — a screen, about an arm's length away — and in one position. A good break simply changes those things. A different distance, a different posture, a different kind of attention.

That's all. It doesn't need to be long or impressive. It just needs to be *different* from what you were doing.

## Five-minute breaks that actually help

**Look far away.** Go to a window and find the most distant thing you can see — a rooftop, a tree, the sky. After a screen, your eyes will thank you. A popular habit is the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.

**Stand up and move a little.** Walk to the kitchen the long way. Roll your shoulders. Reach up, then fold down. Nothing that needs a towel afterwards — just a reminder to your body that it isn't a chair.

**Drink some water.** Refill your glass. It's a tiny act of looking after yourself, and it gets you moving.

**Step outside.** Even two minutes of fresh air and daylight can shift your mood. Feel the temperature. Notice what the sky is doing.

**Do something with your hands.** Water a plant. Wash a mug. Tidy one small surface. Simple physical tasks let your mind rest while your hands stay busy.

**Do nothing at all.** Sit, breathe out slowly, and let your mind wander. It feels unproductive, and that's exactly the point. A wandering mind is often where ideas quietly connect.

> **Try this at your next break:** Leave your phone face down on your desk. Walk to a window, look at the farthest thing you can see, and take three slow breaths. Then refill your water and come back.

## Longer breaks

After an hour or two of focus, take something longer — fifteen to thirty minutes. Eat something real, away from your desk if you can. Go for a short walk. Talk to someone about something that isn't work.

These longer pauses are what make a whole day sustainable. The goal isn't to work more hours; it's to still feel like yourself at the end of them.

## Letting a break be a break

The hardest part of resting is often the guilt — the voice that says you haven't earned it yet. But rest isn't a prize for finishing; it's part of how good work happens. You don't need permission. You're allowed to stop for a few minutes.
