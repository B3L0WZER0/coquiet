When you work from home, or carry work around on your phone, the workday doesn't always end. It just fades — into dinner, into the evening, into lying awake at eleven thinking about an email you forgot to send.

A shutdown ritual is a small, deliberate way of saying: *that's enough for today.* It takes about ten minutes, and it can make your evenings feel much more like evenings.

## Why unfinished work follows you home

Our minds are good at holding on to unfinished things. Open loops — the half-written reply, the task you meant to start — keep tapping us on the shoulder long after we've stopped working.

The good news is that they don't need to be *finished* to go quiet. Research on unfinished goals suggests that simply making a concrete plan for them is often enough to settle the mind. You don't have to do it now. You just have to know when you will.

That's what a shutdown ritual gives you.

## A ten-minute shutdown

1. **Write down what's still open.** A quick list of anything unfinished or on your mind. Get it out of your head and onto paper.
2. **Choose tomorrow's first task.** Just one. Write it at the top of tomorrow's page, so the morning begins with a decision already made.
3. **Close the loops you can.** Any two-minute replies or tiny jobs? Do them now. Anything bigger goes on the list.
4. **Tidy one thing.** Close your tabs. Clear your desk. Leave your space the way you'd like to find it.
5. **Say that it's done.** Some people say a phrase out loud — "that's the day" — or close the laptop with a little ceremony. It sounds silly. It works.

## Mark the change

After the ritual, do something that clearly belongs to the evening. Change your clothes. Walk around the block. Put the kettle on. Play a different kind of music. If you work where you live, a small physical change helps your mind notice that the room has changed purpose.

And if a work thought pops up later, you can tell it, kindly: *it's on the list. See you tomorrow.*

> **Try this tonight:** Before you stop working, take five minutes to write down what's still open and choose one first task for tomorrow. Then close your laptop and do one thing that feels like evening.

## The other end of the morning

If you've read about [the one-line morning](/journal/one-line-morning-routine), you might notice the two fit together. The shutdown ritual picks tomorrow's first task; the morning line starts the day with a clear head. Bookends, really — a quiet way in and a quiet way out.
